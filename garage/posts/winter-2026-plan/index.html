The winter plan: steering, suspension, and brakes

November 16, 2026

The goal for this winter is one thing: get the Corvette safe to drive by spring 2027. The engine is ready. What's underneath it isn't. So this winter, everything that steers, stops, or holds the car up gets rebuilt or replaced.

Steering

The biggest item is a Borgeson power steering box conversion. The stock C3 setup uses a control valve on the steering linkage, and mine moves around enough that its hydraulic lines rub. Rather than keep patching it, I'm replacing the whole system with a modern integral box. A Ridetech reinforcement brace ties the box to the frame.

Along with it: new Moog tie rod ends, adjusting sleeves, and a greaseable idler arm.

Front suspension

  • Moog upper and lower ball joints
  • Zip offset upper control arm shaft kit
  • Energy Suspension Hyper-Flex master bushing kit in black poly
  • Moog heavy-duty front coil springs
  • Bilstein B6 shocks
  • Addco 1-1/4 in. front sway bar
  • New control arm bumpers
  • Front wheel bearings and seals, repacked and rebuilt

Rear suspension

I'm rebuilding the trailing arms myself and moving up to 3 in. half shafts with 5/16 in. flange bolts, with new trailing arm pivot bolts and Bilstein B6 shocks in the back.

Body mounts

Fresh body mounts all around. I went with rubber, OE style, rather than poly. The suspension can be stiff; the body mounts don't need to be.

Brakes

A full refresh: Raybestos R-Line calipers with stainless sleeves at all four corners, new rotors and pads, new front and rear hoses, and new parking brake cables. I'm keeping the vacuum booster. While the engine is breaking in, a solid street setup is the right call.

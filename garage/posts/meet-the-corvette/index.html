Meet the Corvette

November 16, 2026

This is a 1981 C3 Corvette. When I got it, it was mostly gutted, which turned out to be a good starting point: there wasn't much original left to be precious about, so I could build it the way I wanted.

The plan is simple to say and long to do. I want a street monster with a toned-down IMSA look, no stickers and no numbers, and I want to drive it, not trailer it to shows. It's a forever car, so there's no rush. I'll do it in stages, a little more each winter, over the next few years.

What's in it now

The heart of it is a BluePrint 396 small-block stroker, carbureted, with a Holley 850 double pumper, Hooker Super Competition headers, and side pipes. Behind it is a shop-rebuilt TH350 with a manual valve body and a 2700 stall converter. That's a temporary transmission; an M22 four-speed is in the long-term plan.

Cooling is a 3-row Ledfoot aluminum radiator with dual SPAL fans, and the front of the engine runs a CVF serpentine drive with a 120 amp alternator. The A/C, radio, and factory heater box are gone. Power steering comes from an electric Volvo pump on a ServTronic controller, and the battery lives behind the driver's seat.

It sits on American Racing Torq Thrust wheels, 15x8, with 275/60R15 Cooper Cobras at all four corners. They clear without rubbing.

I wired the whole car myself with a custom harness. The engine has fired, but the car hasn't moved under its own power yet.

Why it isn't driving yet

Everything under the car is original or close to it, and a 396 deserves better than 45-year-old bushings and a steering box whose control valve moves around. Before I put miles on it, the steering, suspension, and brakes all get redone. That's this winter's project, and the next post covers the whole list.

Where it's going

After it's safe and driving, the order is bodywork and paint (keeping the current color), a proper interior with a roll bar and custom dash, then the manual swap and a built rear end, then wider wheels and a widebody, and finally engine upgrades. The full stage plan is on the build sheet.

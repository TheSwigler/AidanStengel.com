Replacing a statewide lawyer referral system

How we replaced the system behind about 10,000 lawyer referrals a year, built in iMIS, after the vendor announced it was shutting down.

RoleScaleTeamStatus
Project lead About 10,000 referrals a year Bar staff, plus one outside firm with 5 consultants Live

The problem

The Virginia Lawyer Referral Service connects people who need a lawyer with attorneys who can help, about 10,000 referrals a year. The vendor whose system it ran on was shutting that system down, with no timeline for when. The service couldn't stop taking referrals, so we needed a replacement before the old one disappeared.

The approach

Instead of buying another vendor product, we built the replacement in iMIS, the platform the Bar already runs on. That kept the referral service's data alongside the rest of the Bar's membership data, under our control. The work was split between our team internally and one outside firm with five consultants.

My role

  • Coordinated the people. Kept referral service staff, our team, and the five consultants working from one plan over about a year and a half.
  • Turned workflows into data. Sat with staff to learn how referrals actually move from first call to assigned lawyer, and translated that into the data structures the new system is built on.
  • Organized the plan. Broke the project into pieces the consultants and our team could build, and tracked them through to launch.
  • Hunted down the data. Found and pulled together the records the new system needed from wherever they actually lived.

How it came together

The hard parts

  • No deadline from the vendor, so the project had to move fast without knowing how much time we really had.
  • Workflows that lived in people's heads had to become fields, tables, and rules before anything could be built.
  • The data needed for the new system wasn't all in one place, and some of it had to be tracked down.
  • Keeping a five-person outside team and internal staff aligned over a long timeline.

The result

The new system is live, and the referral service runs on it today, inside the same platform as the rest of the Bar's member data.
